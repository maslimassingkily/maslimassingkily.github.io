Conservation genomics & population assessment

Indonesia · Indo-Pacific · current research direction
A research direction using targeted nuclear genomic approaches to improve resolution of population structure, connectivity, and management units for threatened turtles.

Overview

This research direction builds on population genetic research in Indonesian and Indo-Pacific turtles and focuses on applying nuclear genomic approaches to improve the resolution of population structure and connectivity. The work aims to generate practical genomic tools that can complement existing mitochondrial DNA datasets and strengthen the evidence available for conservation planning.

Rationale

Nuclear genomic markers can provide higher-resolution information on population structure and connectivity than single mitochondrial loci alone. Developing practical genomic approaches is therefore important for resolving population relationships and informing conservation decisions for threatened turtle populations.

Approach

Targeted SNP genotyping, including GT-seq approaches, provides a practical framework for developing species-specific genomic panels and applying them to conservation samples. The approach is designed to generate high-resolution nuclear genetic data while remaining suitable for application across conservation and research programmes.

Conservation application

The long-term goal is to resolve population structure and connectivity and strengthen evidence-based management units across Indonesian and Indo-Pacific turtle populations.
