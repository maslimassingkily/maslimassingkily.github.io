About

Dr. Maslim As-Singkily is a conservation scientist with 15+ years of experience in biodiversity conservation, ecological monitoring, molecular ecology, and conservation genetics across Indonesia and the Indo-Pacific, with a particular focus on terrestrial, freshwater, and marine turtles. He holds a PhD in Animal Ecology from Wageningen University & Research, the Netherlands.

Maslim's work integrates field ecology, molecular ecology, and conservation planning to support evidence-based management of threatened species and ecosystems. Over a career, the throughline has been the same: pair rigorous science with the people and institutions who can act on it.

He has collaborated extensively with NGOs, government agencies, research institutions, and international partners — translating ecological and genetic data into species assessments, policy recommendations, management plans, and on-the-ground conservation outcomes across Indonesia.

2021 — 2026Indonesia

Leatherback turtle populations in Indonesia

This project integrated long-term ecological monitoring with environmental DNA (eDNA), population genetics, and policy analysis to better understand leatherback turtle (Dermochelys coriacea) population trends, genetic connectivity, and conservation governance in Indonesia. The research contributes to efforts to reassess the Northeast Indian Ocean Regional Management Unit (RMU), improve understanding of population connectivity, and identify underrepresented rookeries that should be prioritised for conservation.

2016 — 2020Rote Island, NTT

Conservation of Rote Island snake-necked turtle

Established in 2016, this species conservation programme supports the recovery of the Rote Island snake-necked turtle (Chelodina mccordi), which was considered extinct in the wild on Rote Island, Indonesia. The programme conducts long-term in situ research to identify and monitor remaining populations and habitats, while also establishing Indonesia’s first assurance colony for the species in Kupang to support future reintroduction through the repatriation of individuals from captive populations in zoos.
