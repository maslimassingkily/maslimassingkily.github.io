My work integrates field ecology, molecular ecology, population genetics, and conservation planning to support evidence-based management of threatened species and ecosystems. Across coastal Aceh, Sumatran leatherback nesting beaches, and turtle landscapes in eastern Indonesia, the common thread has been linking rigorous science with the people and institutions able to act on it.

I have worked with NGOs, government agencies, research institutions, and international partners on biodiversity monitoring, turtle conservation, conservation policy, species assessments, and applied research. My research interests include turtle population structure and connectivity, eDNA-based monitoring, conservation genomics, ecological modelling, and the use of science to strengthen conservation management.

I completed my PhD in Marine Animal Ecology at Wageningen University & Research, where my doctoral research focused on the ecology, genetics, and conservation of leatherback turtles in Sumatra.

Based in Indonesia · Open to research, conservation, consultancy, teaching, and international collaboration.
